OPERATOR GUIDE

READING AND OPERATING THE FLEET CONSOLE

01 READING THE DASHBOARD

The dashboard is the default wall view. The status bar across the top shows fleet totals (idle / printing / error / offline), the console clock, and a LINK indicator for the browser’s connection to the Pi — if LINK is red, what you see may be stale.

Each printer is a bezel-framed panel, headed by the printer’s name in large glowing letters. The name stays in the same place whichever face is showing, so you can find a printer from across the room. Panels cycle automatically every ~10 seconds between three faces: the status readout, an ambient scanner animation (any printer not in ERROR or OFFLINE, printing ones included), and — for one printer at a time — the live camera slot, which rotates through the fleet. A panel is never more than three turns away from its status readout. The state lamp in each panel’s header never cycles away, so fleet state stays readable from across the room. Tap any panel to open its detail console.

THE LAYER STACK

While a printer is printing or paused, its animation turns cycle through the layer stack, the progress cube (below), and the scanner animations. The layer stack is a picture of the job itself. Each block is one layer. Finished layers stack bottom-up, left to right, as glowing cyan blocks; the layer being printed glows amber and pulses, then flashes white and settles into the stack when it completes. While the printer is paused, that block turns violet and stops pulsing. The dashed rounded rectangle is the whole job: its size is fixed by the total layer count, and the faint outlines inside it are the layers still to come. The readout at the bottom left gives the layer and percentage. On very tall prints each block stands for several layers, and a 1 BLOCK = N LAYERS note appears in the corner.

THE PROGRESS CUBE

A tapered wedge meter on the left fills with the job’s progress, with the percentage above it and a bright line and marker at the fill edge. On the right, a glowing wireframe cube spins inside a reticle while the printer prints. When the printer is paused the cube holds still and the markers turn violet. Idle printers show it too, as one of their resting faces: the meter reads STANDBY and the cube drifts slowly.

On a wide screen, such as a fullscreen lab display, panels are laid out two across, and two rows fill the screen. Each panel takes about a quarter of the display, and its text and meters scale up to match. A third printer sits bottom-left, leaving the slot for a fourth; a fifth or more scrolls to a third row. Narrow windows and phones fit as many smaller panels across as there is room for.

At that size, a printing, paused, or ERROR panel’s status readout also shows a large progress reticle: an open arc of segments that fill in steps as the job advances, with the percentage in the middle. It is amber while printing, violet when paused, and red when an error is holding the job. Idle panels center their readout instead.

When a job finishes, that panel — or its detail console, if you have it open — takes over for 45 seconds with a completion sequence: the part is inspected on a turntable, meters cap out, a seal draws itself and stamps FINISHED. Normal cycling resumes afterwards. A cancelled job does not trigger it, and a fault always wins the panel — an error interrupts the celebration rather than hiding behind it.

02 PRINTER STATES

IDLE Steady cyan. Online and ready. Readout shows temperatures and the last job.
PRINTING Pulsing amber. Shows file name, layer count, nozzle/bed temperatures, time remaining, and progress.
PAUSED Steady violet. The job is held and can be resumed from the detail view.
ERROR Blinking red — and the entire panel bezel flashes red in sync. The readout lists HMS code(s). Error panels never show the scanner animation: they hold the fault readout, giving way only to the live camera on their turn so you can see the failed print.
OFFLINE Dark lamp. No MQTT link — check power, network, and the printer’s network settings. After 30 seconds the panel asks for the printer’s address (see Lost printers).

03 CAMERA VIEWS

One camera relay runs at a time; on the dashboard the live slot rotates through the fleet automatically, and opening a detail view pins that printer’s camera. What the feed looks like depends on the hardware:

  • X1 Carbon / H2D — real video (RTSPS), relayed at a reduced frame rate to keep the Pi comfortable.
  • A1 / A1 Mini — the printer’s chamber camera provides roughly one frame per second. The slideshow feel is the hardware’s design, not a fault.
◈ NOTE

The white ● LIVE tag is an indicator, not an alarm — red is reserved exclusively for error signaling. A grey NO SIGNAL tag means liveview is unavailable for that printer; status and controls still work.

04 CONTROLS

All controls live in the detail console (tap a panel). Buttons grey out when they don’t apply to the printer’s current state. To return to the fleet, use ◂ FLEET OVERVIEW at the top left, the ▦ FLEET button in the bottom-right corner, or press Esc.

On a wide screen the detail console fills it: the live camera takes the left column above the controls, and the right column opens with the same progress reticle as the fleet panels, beside the file name, time left, and layer, followed by the remaining readings and the AMS slots.

CONTROLBEHAVIOR
⏸ PAUSE / ▶ RESUMEHolds or resumes the running job. The button swaps depending on state. Resume also clears a recoverable ERROR (e.g. after feeding new filament).
■ STOPAborts the job. Asks for confirmation first (see caution below).
☼ LIGHTToggles the chamber light — useful before judging a camera view.
▲ START PRINT…Opens the file dialog: pick a sliced .3mf already on the printer’s SD card, or upload one from your device. Selecting a file arms the START button.
▲ CAUTION

STOP cannot be undone — a stopped job restarts from zero. The console always asks for confirmation before sending it.

◈ GOOD PRACTICE

Before starting a print remotely, use the camera to confirm the bed is clear. The console cannot see what the camera cannot.

05 LOST PRINTERS

A printer that has been unreachable for 30 seconds usually has a new address from the network. Its panel stops cycling and asks, and a white NEEDS ADDRESS chip appears in the status bar:

  • ⌨ ENTER ADDRESS — type the printer’s current address. It’s on the printer’s own screen under Settings → Network. Use the on-screen keypad or a keyboard, then CONNECT.
  • ⌖ AUTO-FIND — let the console search the network for it.

If nobody answers within 2 minutes, the console starts auto-find by itself, so a printer that moves overnight is found without anyone at the screen. While it searches, the panel shows SEARCHING THE NETWORK… with the next attempt’s countdown, and ENTER ADDRESS stays available. From the detail view, ⌨ SET ADDRESS… opens the same dialog at any time while the printer is offline.

Before accepting an address, the console tests it by logging in with that printer’s own access code:

RESULTMEANING
✓ CONNECTEDThe printer is there. The console reconnects at once and, unless you clear SAVE TO CONFIG.JSON, keeps the address for the next restart.
IT ISN’T <PRINTER>Another printer answered at that address and refused this one’s access code. Check the address on the right printer’s screen.
NOTHING ANSWEREDNo printer at that address. Check the number, or use AUTO-FIND.
