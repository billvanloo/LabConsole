PRINT COMPLETE CELEBRATIONS

Three options for the flourish a panel shows when a job finishes. Each runs 45 seconds, then the panel returns to its normal face rotation. This revision is rebuilt from your Galaxy's Edge photographs rather than from a general idea of the style.

WHAT THE REFERENCES CHANGED

The tally board is the authentic completion idiom. Your third photo shows the ride's own end-of-run panel: Aurebesh header, hexagonal emblem with a dot cluster, bold label-and-value rows, and a boxed total. Option A is built directly on it.

Meters are tapered wedges, not blocks. The readouts in your first photo ramp from thin to thick with a green tick comb over the top and an amber end-cap — not the rectangular segments I used in rev 1. Reticles are open arcs, not closed rings.

The radial histogram beats a generic burst. Your second photo's polar chart — filled wedges and thin spikes over a scanline-hatched disc — is now option B.

Implementation notes

TRIGGER

The frontend already sees every state change over the WebSocket, so completion is detectable client-side: previous observed state printing, new state idle. No backend change and no new field in view().

Only fires when a previous state was actually observed, so a page load or reconnect never triggers a false celebration.

PLACEMENT

A new face in panelFaceFor, above the camera and resting faces for its 45 seconds — but still below error, so a fault always wins the panel.

Option A needs real job figures. layer, total_layers and file already arrive in the payload; runtime and material would need either a small addition to view() or dropping those two rows.

THE ORANGE QUESTION

The references use orange and red heavily — video borders in photo 1, most of the polar chart in photo 2. CLAUDE.md reserves them for faults, so all three options push amber and mint instead.

That is the one place these deliberately diverge from the source. Worth deciding explicitly: relaxing it would read as more faithful, at the cost of the error signal's exclusivity.
